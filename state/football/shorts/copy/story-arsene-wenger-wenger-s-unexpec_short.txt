When Wenger Walked Away From Arsenal. The French “Professeur” arrived at Highbury in 1996, a sceptical outsider who rewrote English football with nutrition, training and global scouting. He became the first foreign manager to clinch a Premier League-FA Cup double in 1998, repeated it in 2002, and delivered the unbeaten 2004 title, a record 49-match league run. He guided Arsenal to their first Champions League final in 2006 and oversaw the move to the Emirates, but the new stadium’s finances sparked a nine-year trophy drought. In the 2010s Wenger added three more FA Cups, becoming the competition’s most successful manager with seven wins, yet the league remained out of reach. By mid-2017 he broke the club’s transfer record for Alexandre Lacazette at £45 million and added Sead Kolašinac on a free. On 6 August Arsenal lifted a seventh Community Shield, beating Chelsea 4-1 on penalties after a 1-1 draw. The season spiralled. In the 2017-18 FA Cup Arsenal fell to Nottingham Forest in the third round, and the league table showed them well outside the top four. Player unrest grew as Mesut Şenyoğlan voiced a desire to leave; Wenger sanctioned his January move to Manchester United, receiving Henrikh Mkhitaryan in a swap. Later that month he splashed £56 million on Pierre-Emerick Aubameyang, a club-record fee. On 20 April Wenger announced he would step down at season’s end. His final home game on 6 May ended in a 5-0 victory over Burnley, a standing ovation and a gold mini-replica of the 2003-04 Invincibles trophy presented as a parting gift. He closed his tenure with a 1-0 away win at Huddersfield Town. Wenger later revealed he wanted to stay until his contract expired, but the board had decided in January to end it. He called the hostility from board and fans “unjustified,” describing his exit as “very hard” and “very brutal.”
