Juventus fell from Serie A royalty to Serie B exile in a single summer, and the drama still echoes today. In 2006 the Calciopoli scandal erupted, dragging Italy’s biggest clubs—Juventus, AC Milan, Lazio, Fiorentina, Arezzo and Reggina—into a legal maelstrom. Prosecutor Stefano Palazzi demanded Juventus be thrown out of Serie A, docked six points and stripped of the 2005 title, while the other clubs faced relegation to Serie B and point penalties. The court’s verdict was uneven. Judge Piero Sandulli declared the 2004-05 championship “not falsified,” yet only Juventus was sentenced to drop to Serie B. The Old Lady began the 2006-07 season with a 30-point deduction, later reduced to nine, yet still won the division and earned promotion at once. The punishment sparked a massive exodus: Cannavaro, Ibrahimović, Thuram, Vieira, Zambrotta and thirty World-Cup players left. Remarkably, Del Piero, Buffon, Camoranesi, Nedved, Trezeguet, Chiellini and Marchisio stayed, branding themselves “the purgatory of Serie B.” Juventus rebuilt, built a new stadium, and later claimed nine straight titles—until Inter and Conte halted the run in 2021. From scandal to resurgence, Juventus proved a true Old Lady never folds.
