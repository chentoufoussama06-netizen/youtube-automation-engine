Leicester City was heading into a historic fairytale, led by an unlikely hero named Jamie Vardy. Once released by Sheffield Wednesday at 16 and earning just £120 a week at Stocksbridge Park Steels, the forward went on a legendary scoring streak. By late 2015, Vardy scored in nine straight league games, including penalties against Bournemouth, Norwich City, and Watford. On 21 November, he scored in a 3–0 win over Newcastle United to match Ruud van Nistelrooy’s record of 10 straight matches. One week later, against Manchester United, Vardy struck again to score in his 11th consecutive Premier League match, breaking Van Nistelrooy's record and etching his name into football history. And the 5000–1 outsiders were now chasing the title. Part three is next.
