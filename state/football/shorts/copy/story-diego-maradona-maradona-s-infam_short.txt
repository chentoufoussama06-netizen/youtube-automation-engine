Diego Maradona’s “Hand of God” – the moment that turned a World Cup quarter-final into legend. Born 30 October 1960, the Argentine left-footed wizard rose to become one of football’s two joint FIFA Player of the Century winners, alongside Pelé. In 1986 he captained Argentina, played every minute, scored five goals and delivered five assists, steering a nation still haunted by the Falklands War toward a dream of glory. The goal was ruled a legitimate strike and instantly christened the “Hand of God”. From the right side Maradona gathered the ball, then sprinted 60 metre (66 yd) past five English defenders, never touching the ball with his right foot. He slotted home with his left, a strike later voted FIFA. com’s “Goal of the Century” in 2002. The whistle blew, Argentina led 2 – 1, and the match ended with that scoreline. Maradona’s double secured a place in World Cup folklore, but the hand-ball sparked endless debate. England’s Glenn Hoddle later admitted Maradona had disguised the foul by flicking his head, while striker Gary Lineker confessed that the second goal was “impossible to score” and called Maradona “the greatest player of all time”. The impact rippled beyond sport. Maradona linked the illicit strike to the Malvinas conflict, calling it “revenge” for Argentine boys killed in the war. The goal lifted a depressed nation, turned a captain into a deity, and cemented a legacy that still divides fans. One moment, two goals, a nation reborn – that’s the Hand of God.
