George Best was one of football's greatest talents, but his ultimate battle was with his own self-destruction. The Northern Irish winger rose to fame at Manchester United, capturing the 1968 European Footballer of the Year award. Known for his incredible pace, balance, and playboy lifestyle, he became a global media celebrity. However, his extravagant lifestyle led to severe alcoholism, a disease that haunted him for the rest of his life. By March 2000, Best was diagnosed with severe liver damage, with his liver functioning at just twenty percent. He received a liver transplant in 2002, but his struggle with alcohol continued. On 3 October 2005, Best was admitted to intensive care in London, suffering from a kidney infection caused by the side effects of his immuno-suppressive drugs. In November, he requested a picture of himself in his hospital bed be published, warning others: "Don't die like me." On 25 November 2005, treatment was stopped, and the fifty-nine-year-old legend died from a lung infection and multiple organ failure. A genius on the pitch, silenced by his demons off it.
