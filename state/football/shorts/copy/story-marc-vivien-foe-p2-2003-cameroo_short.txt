Marc-Vivien Foé’s story didn’t end on the pitch that night – the drama moved to Paris. Cameroon faced France in the 2003 Confederations Cup final, still mourning the semi-final loss of their midfield anchor. As the teams entered the Stade de France, captains Rigobert Song and Marcel Desailly each held a picture of Foé. A minute of silence unfolded, then both squads linked arms in the centre circle. Every Cameroonian wore his number 17 on their warm-up shirts, his name stitched on the back. Foé was posthumously awarded the Bronze Ball as the tournament’s third-most valuable player. His legacy lived on, louder than any silence.
