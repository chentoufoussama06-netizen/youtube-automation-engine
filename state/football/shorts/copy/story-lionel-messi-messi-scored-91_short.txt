When Messi Broke the Impossible Goal Record. The stage was set: a new season, a hungry club, and a historic benchmark that seemed untouchable. The campaign began with Messi already a proven scorer, but the pressure mounted as the year unfolded. Messi kept his pace, contributing to over 40 % of Barcelona’s goals by season’s end, a dramatic rise from the 24 % he supplied during the 2008–09 treble. When the clock struck the final months of 2012, Messi had already surpassed the German legend’s record. He finished the year with a staggering 91 goals in all competitions for Barcelona and Argentina, a figure that eclipsed Müller’s long-standing record. The achievement crowned him with his fourth FIFA Ballon d’Or, making him the first player ever to win the award four times. Barcelona responded to his dominance by extending his contract through 2018 and handing him the captain’s armband for the first time in a league match against Rayo Vallecano. The team clinched La Liga again, matching Real Madrid’s 100-point record from the previous season, while Messi topped the scoring charts with 60 goals in all competitions, including 46 in La Liga, earning a third consecutive European Golden Shoe. Messi’s record-breaking year reshaped football history. It proved a single player could rewrite the sport’s statistical boundaries and cemented the concept of “Messidependencia,” the idea that Barcelona’s fortunes hinged on his brilliance. The feat raised the bar for every striker who followed, turning a once-unthinkable target into a new standard of excellence. History remembers the number 91 as Messi’s ultimate statement.
