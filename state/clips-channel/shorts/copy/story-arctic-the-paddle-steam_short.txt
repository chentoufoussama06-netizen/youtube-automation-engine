Fog swallowed the Arctic, then a collision sealed fate. Four hundred souls aboard faced a nightmarish disaster. On September 27, 1854, Arctic struck the French Vesta fifty miles off Newfoundland. Only six lifeboats for 180 places sparked panic; women and children were left. From 400 aboard, just 24 male passengers and 64 crew survived; all women perished. Could any rule have saved them?
