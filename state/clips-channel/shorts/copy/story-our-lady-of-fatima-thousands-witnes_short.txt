On October 13, 1917, Our Lady of Fátima reportedly gathered tens of thousands of people to witness a terrifying solar miracle. Months earlier, three Catholic shepherd children near Fátima, Portugal—Lúcia Santos and her cousins Francisco and Jacinta Marto—reported visions of an angel, followed by apparitions of the Virgin Mary. The children claimed the Lady promised that on October 13, she would reveal her identity and perform a miracle so that all may believe. The prophecies drew intense controversy. Local secular and religious authorities criticized the children, and a provisional administrator even took them into custody, suspecting political motives against the secular First Portuguese Republic. Yet, when the promised day arrived, a massive crowd gathered in Fátima. What happened next lasted approximately ten minutes. Witnesses reported extraordinary solar activity. They saw the Sun appear to dance or zig-zag in the sky, advance toward the Earth, and emit multicolored light. The event sparked immediate debate. Skeptics pointed out that not everyone present saw the phenomenon, and witness accounts contained considerable inconsistencies. A photograph later published by a Vatican newspaper in 1951, often mistaken for the actual event, was actually taken years later in a different town. Some skeptics argued that people were anticipating a solar event, even darkening pieces of glass beforehand to watch the Sun. Others noted that similar solar movements had been observed elsewhere by people staring at the Sun under analogous circumstances. However, many believers and investigators found the event deeply credible. A local priest conducting a canonical investigation was particularly convinced by concurring testimonies from secular reporters, government officials, and skeptics. One analysis of crowd photographs even suggested the presence of a real, luminous "silver sun" object over Fátima. In October 1930, Bishop José da Silva officially declared the miracle worthy of belief, permitting the cult of Our Lady of Fátima within the Catholic Church. A promised sign became a historic phenomenon.
