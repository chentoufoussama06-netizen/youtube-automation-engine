In 1932, the Australian military fought the Emu War—and lost to twenty thousand birds. After World War I, veteran farmers in Western Australia faced falling wheat prices and thousands of invading emus ravaging their crops. The Minister of Defence deployed Royal Australian Artillery soldiers armed with Lewis guns. Major Meredith led the operation in November 1932. But the birds split into alert, highly mobile groups. Machine guns jammed, and the emus ran like tanks. Media ridicule forced a temporary withdrawal. A second attempt claimed kills, but the operation proved uneconomic. The government abandoned the cull, leaving the emus victorious.
