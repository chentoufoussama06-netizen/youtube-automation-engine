Molasses Flood Victim—watch the terrifying 1919 Boston disaster unfold in under a minute. On January 15, 1919, a 50-foot steel tank at the Purity Distilling Company on Commercial Street burst, unleashing 2. The syrup surged at an estimated 35 mph, forming a 25-foot wall that ripped through the North End, buckling the elevated railway, toppling buildings and sweeping a streetcar off its tracks. The sticky tide flooded several blocks to two-three feet deep, trapping people, horses and dogs. Twenty-one lives were lost and about 150 were injured. Boston still smelled of molasses for decades.
