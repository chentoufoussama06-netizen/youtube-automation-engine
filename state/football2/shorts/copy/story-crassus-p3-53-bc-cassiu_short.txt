Following the disaster at Carrhae, Marcus Licinius Crassus was dead. Now, ten thousand surviving Roman soldiers faced a perilous retreat across enemy lands. Quaestor Gaius Cassius Longinus stepped up to lead the survivors all the way back to Syria. He then governed the province for two years, successfully defending it against Orodes' son Pacorus. Cassius resisted a siege at Antioch, and when the Parthians retreated, he ambushed them at Antigonea, killing their second-in-command Osaces. Years later, in 44 BC, he played a key role in assassinating Julius Caesar.
