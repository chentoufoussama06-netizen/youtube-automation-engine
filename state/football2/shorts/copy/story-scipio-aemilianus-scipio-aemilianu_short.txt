In early 146 BC, Rome launched its final assault on Carthage. Public support had lifted usual age restrictions to make Scipio Aemilianus commander in Africa. After a nearly three-year siege, he had blocked supplies with an immense mole and built a brick structure dominating the city wall. Over one week, the Romans systematically destroyed Carthage and killed its inhabitants. On the last day, they took 50,000 prisoners and sold them into slavery.
