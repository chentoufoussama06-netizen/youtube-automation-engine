Thirty-eight war elephants climbed into the freezing Alps. In 218 BC, Hannibal led them across the mountains to invade Italy, sparking the Second Punic War. His army crossed the Pyrenees and reached the Rhône by September. Yet the harsh mountain conditions proved brutal. Almost none of the elephants survived the journey, and he lost nearly half his force before reaching the Italian plains.
