In 53 BC, Rome’s wealthiest man, Marcus Licinius Crassus, led seven legions into the desert for ultimate military glory. Instead, he marched straight into disaster at Carrhae. The Parthian general Surena surrounded the vulnerable Roman heavy infantry with mobile horse archers and cataphracts. Crassus watched his army crushingly defeated and saw his son’s head placed on a spear. When truce negotiations turned violent, Crassus was killed. Rome suffered twenty thousand dead.
