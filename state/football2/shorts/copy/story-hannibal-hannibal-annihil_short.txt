Eighty-six thousand Roman and allied troops marched to crush Hannibal at Cannae. Roman confidence was soaring after a victory in a preliminary skirmish, but their deeper infantry formation left them vulnerable. Hannibal used the double envelopment tactic, surrounding his enemy in one of history's most lethal days of fighting. Only about fifteen thousand Romans survived. The defeat led Capua and several Italian city-states to defect to Carthage.
