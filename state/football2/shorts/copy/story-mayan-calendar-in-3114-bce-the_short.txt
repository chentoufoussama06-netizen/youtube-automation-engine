August 11, 3114 BC marked the beginning of time. The ancient Maya created the Long Count calendar, but how did they track centuries without losing their place? According to Maya tradition, the deity Itzamna brought this calendrical system to the people. To record history beyond a 52-year cycle, they used a linear, positional notation system tallying days from a mythological starting point. Instead of a standard base-10 decimal scheme, they counted days using a modified base-20 vigesimal system. Each position represented twenty times the unit before it. However, they made a critical exception for the second-order place value. Instead of twenty times twenty, which equals 400 days, it represented 18 times 20, or 360 days. This calculation aligned their system far more closely with the solar year. Their setup also tracked higher-order cycles, stretching all the way up to the piktun, kalabtun, kʼinchiltun, and alautun. Today, this ancient system still matters because it allows dates to be extended infinitely far into the past or future.
