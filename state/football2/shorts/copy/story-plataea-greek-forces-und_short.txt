In August 479 BC, near Plataea, an Achaemenid army led by Mardonius faced the Spartan-led Hellenic alliance. Mardonius hurried with the Persian left wing to attack the retreating Greeks. The Greeks fought a pitched battle using hoplites against Persian light infantry. Konijnendijk and Bardunias note that the Persians lost because of overspecialization in a battle of shield walls. After these twin victories of Plataea and Mycale, the second Persian invasion of Greece was over.
