When Archimedes was killed during the 212 BC siege, Syracuse still resisted. The population fell back to the fortified inner citadel, holding off the Romans. Proconsul Marcellus cut off supplies to the reduced area, subjecting the defenders to eight months of severe hunger. Then, an Iberian captain named Moeriscus opened a gate near the Fountains of Arethusa. Marcellus gave the city to plunder. The angered Romans rampaged through the citadel, slaughtering many Syracusans and enslaving most of the rest.
