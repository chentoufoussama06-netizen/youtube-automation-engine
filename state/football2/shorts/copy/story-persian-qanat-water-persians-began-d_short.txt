Persian qanat water tunnels were buried deep to survive the desert heat. In these dry lands, how could populations transport ground water across long distances without losing it all to intense heat? Persian qanat technology solved this by moving ground water through subterranean tunnels, keeping the flow entirely below ground to prevent evaporation and contamination. First, ancient Iranians excavated underground galleries linked to the surface through vertical shafts, which allowed access for repairs. To ensure proper flow, miners filled the tunnels with coarser material than the surrounding geological formations, knowing water movement depends on sediment grain size. When the water remained underground, drawn via wells or animal-driven Persian wells, its airflow cooled subterranean summer rooms in nearby buildings. To guarantee a fair distribution for agricultural irrigation, Persians measured the water shares using water clocks as early as 500 BCE. This system remains essential. Modern UNESCO recognition and functional ancient sites prove that a three-thousand-year-old subterranean design still provides reliable water to thousands of people today.
