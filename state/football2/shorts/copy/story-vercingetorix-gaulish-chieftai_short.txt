In early 52 BC, Vercingetorix, whose Gaulish name means supreme king of warriors, launched a massive revolt against Julius Caesar. The Arvernian king united the tribes of Gaul to stop the invading Roman army. Using scorched-earth tactics, he harried Caesar’s forces and even handed him a defeat at Gergovia. But at the siege of Alesia, trapped in the stronghold amid terrible starvation, Vercingetorix was forced to surrender in September 52 BC. He was imprisoned in Rome for six years, then executed in 46 BC.
