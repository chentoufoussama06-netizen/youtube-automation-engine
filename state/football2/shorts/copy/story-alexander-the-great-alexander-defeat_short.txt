In 331 BC, Alexander the Great faced King Darius III at Gaugamela to conquer the Achaemenid Empire. Alexander marched his Macedonian army along a northern route to find the Persian forces. Although outnumbered, Alexander possessed highly trained troops armed with six-meter sarissa pikes. During the battle, Alexander rode toward the right flank, drawing the Persian cavalry away to create a critical gap in their line. Darius ordered his left wing to block him, triggering a brutal cavalry engagement. Seizing the moment, Alexander formed his units into a giant wedge and charged directly into the weakened Persian center. The wedge smashed through the royal guard. Facing total ruin, Darius broke and fled on horseback. Alexander halted his pursuit to rescue his encircled left wing, but the blow was already struck. The Persian Empire was shattered forever.
