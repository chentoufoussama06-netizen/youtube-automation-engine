In 480 BC, King Leonidas led 300 elite Spartans into a deadly bottleneck to stop the massive Persian Empire. Xerxes invaded Greece with up to 300,000 soldiers. To block them, Leonidas marched north to the narrow pass of Thermopylae with around 7,000 Greeks. For two days of direct battle, the Greeks stood shoulder to shoulder. Their giant shields and long spears cut the Persian waves to ribbons. Xerxes stood up from his seat three times in sheer disbelief. Then came the ultimate betrayal. A local resident named Ephialtes showed the Persians a mountain path behind the Greek lines. Knowing they were outflanked, Leonidas dismissed the bulk of his army. He chose to stay and fight to the death with his 300 Spartans and 700 loyal Thespians. They fought until the very end, transforming Leonidas and his men into history's ultimate martyrs.
