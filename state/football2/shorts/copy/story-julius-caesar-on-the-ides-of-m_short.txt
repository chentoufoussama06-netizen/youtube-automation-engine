Julius Caesar was surrounded by sixty conspirators inside the Senate House. They claimed to defend the Roman Republic, but their plot risked plunging Rome into civil war. On the Ides of March, 44 BC, senators pulled down Caesar's toga and attacked him with daggers. Stabbing him 23 times, they left his corpse for his slaves to collect. Was their bloody act truly a defense of the Republic?
