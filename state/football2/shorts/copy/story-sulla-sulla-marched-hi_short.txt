No general had ever marched his own legions directly on Rome. In 88 BC, Consul Lucius Cornelius Sulla faced a crisis that threatened his dignity and career. The Senate had awarded him command against Mithridates, but a political deal between Marius and Publius Sulpicius revoked it. Refusing to lose his command, Sulla took control of his army. He marched on Rome, violently expelling Marius, Sulpicius, and their allies from the city. He seized Rome through force.
