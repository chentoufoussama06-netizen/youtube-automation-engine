The Great Library of Alexandria faced tragic disaster in 48 BC during Caesar’s Civil War. Alexandria was regarded as the ancient capital of knowledge, holding up to 700,000 scrolls. But when Julius Caesar was besieged, his soldiers set fire to docked Egyptian ships to block the fleet of Ptolemy XIII. The flames spread from the docks into the city, burning storehouses of books. Seneca quotes Livy that 40,000 scrolls were destroyed.
