Six years. That is how long Vercingetorix spent imprisoned before his execution. Starvation at Alesia forced the Gauls to debate surrender. Vercingetorix suddenly appeared on horseback, circled Caesar's tribunal, cast down his arms, and threw himself at Caesar's feet. He surrendered hoping for clemency based on past friendship. Instead, Caesar imprisoned him in the Tullianum for six years. In 46 BCE, Vercingetorix was publicly displayed in Caesar's triumph, then executed in the Tullianum.
