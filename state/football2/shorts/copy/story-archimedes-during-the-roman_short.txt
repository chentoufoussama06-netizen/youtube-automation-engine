In 212 BC, the brilliant mathematician Archimedes was slain in Syracuse—by a soldier ordered to spare him. During the Second Punic War, Roman forces under proconsul Marcus Claudius Marcellus besieged the city. Archimedes defended Syracuse with deadly inventions like the massive Claw of Archimedes. When Roman soldiers finally breached the city, Archimedes continued his studies at home. A soldier interrupted his work, and Archimedes coarsely told him to leave. Unaware of the inventor's identity, the soldier killed him on the spot. A genius destroyed by sheer ignorance.
