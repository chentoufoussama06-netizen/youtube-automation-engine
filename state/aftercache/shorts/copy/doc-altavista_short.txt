How a DEC project became the 1990s' top search engine? AltaVista launched on December 15 1995, emerging from Digital Equipment Corporation researchers who wanted to showcase their hardware by making the growing public network searchable. Paul Flaherty conceived the idea, Louis Monier built the fast multi‑threaded crawler named Scooter, and Michael Burrows created the TurboVista index that delivered the first full‑text, Boolean searchable web index. The service attracted 300,000 hits on its opening day and surged to more than 80 million daily queries by 1997, cementing its place among the era’s most visited sites. In 1996 AltaVista became Yahoo’s exclusive search provider, and in 1997 it added Babel Fish, a web‑based translation tool.
