The US Army needed a light military vehicle for rough terrain. In 1941, the World War II jeep went into production to meet that critical need. Willys-Overland chief engineer Delmar "Barney" Roos modified the design to use the heavy but powerful "Go Devil" engine. That decision won Willys the initial production contract. Because the US War Department required vast numbers quickly, Willys licensed its plans to Ford to build additional units. Together, they produced about 640,000 Jeeps for the war effort. Nearly thirty percent of all production went to Great Britain and the Soviet Red Army. A simple military four-by-four became a global legend.
