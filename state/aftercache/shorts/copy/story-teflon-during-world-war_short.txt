Teflon was accidentally discovered by Roy Plunkett when a cylinder of gas stopped flowing. The chemists sawed the bottle apart and found the interior coated with a slippery white material. During World War II, DuPont used it for the Manhattan Project to handle reactive uranium. By 1948, DuPont was producing over 910,000 kilograms of Teflon per year, later marketed as a non-stick coating for cookware.
