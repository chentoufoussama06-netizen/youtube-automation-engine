Duct Tape Was Invented to Keep Ammo Dry in WWII. Ordnance worker Vesta Stoudt wrote Roosevelt in 1943 demanding a seal. Johnson & Johnson’s Revolite made a waterproof, rubber-based cloth tape for boxes. The olive-drab strips held, kept ammo dry, and became the post-war household staple. From battlefield seal to every garage drawer, it’s still called duck tape.
