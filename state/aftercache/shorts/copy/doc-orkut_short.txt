Why did Google move Orkut’s control to Brazil’s Belo Horizonte? Orkut, the Google‑owned social network created by Turkish engineer Orkut Büyükkökten, launched quietly on January 22 2004 and quickly became a gathering place for friends and strangers alike. By 2008 the site ranked among the most visited webpages in India and Brazil, prompting Google to place full operational control of the service in Brazil’s Belo Horizonte to address the massive local user base and emerging legal concerns. The platform’s early features let members rate friends as “Trustworthy,” “Cool,” or “Sexy,” add anyone to a “Crush List,” and view any public profile unless blocked, fostering a sense of open community that resonated especially in those two countries.
