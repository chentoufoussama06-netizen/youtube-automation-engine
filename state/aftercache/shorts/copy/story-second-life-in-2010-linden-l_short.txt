Second Life burst onto the scene in 2003, promising a fully user-crafted 3D world where “Residents” could live, work and trade without any built-in objectives. By 2013 it boasted roughly one million regular users, its virtual economy humming with a Linden Dollar that could be exchanged for real cash. The boom faltered after 2009, and in 2010 Linden Lab announced a 30 percent staff layoff, citing a steep decline in growth. The cut marked the end of the platform’s early surge, sealing its transition from meteoric rise to steady-state niche. The virtual world never looked back.
