How a Console Destroyed Its Own Company. It launched with a massive US marketing push, yet sales slipped as PlayStation 2 loomed. By 2000 Sega needed five million US units; only three million sold. Price cuts and cash rebates deepened losses, culminating in a ¥51. 7 billion ($417. On January 31, Sega announced the Dreamcast would end March 31, slashing the price to $49. Nine-point-one million consoles shipped, but the dream died with the final unit signed by all nine internal studios. The console fell, but its legacy lives on.
