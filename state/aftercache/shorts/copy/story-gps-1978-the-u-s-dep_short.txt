A single satellite changed everything: GPS launched in 1978. What if the world had no way to locate itself? The U.S. Department of Defense sent the first Block-I satellite aloft that year. By 1993 the full 24-satellite constellation became operational, delivering precise navigation to every user. That gamble now guides military missions, civilian travel, and countless devices worldwide. Could we ever find our way without it?
